# The questions, and how to read the answers

Send these in one email, in writing, after the desk check — never before. The research usually answers three or four of them, and asking for something you already found wastes credibility you'll want later.

Ordered by how fast they end the conversation. If only three go out, send the first three.

---

### 1. Last year's post-event report — registered vs. verified attended, plus the breakdown by job title, seniority and company size

The question that separates an event business from a lead-gen operation. Real organizers have this as a PDF.

- **Good:** a PDF the same day, distinguishing registered from attended, specific enough to be unflattering in places.
- **Walk away:** "we can go over the numbers on our call," or one headline figure with nothing behind it.

### 2. Which two past sponsors at this tier can I call, and can you introduce me?

Trivially cheap for a real event, impossible for a fake one. Ask the reference about meetings booked, not impressions. A sponsor who came back for a second year is the most valuable reference on the list.

- **Good:** two names and contact details, offered without hedging.
- **Walk away:** logos instead of humans, or "our sponsors prefer not to be contacted."

### 3. Please send the rate card and standard contract before we schedule anything

Published tiers are the norm. Withholding price until a call exists to price-discriminate and apply pressure.

- **Good:** a prospectus with per-tier deliverables, plus a contract with cancellation, force-majeure and refund terms.
- **Walk away:** price only after a call, or a number that moves when you hesitate. Discount codes and "two slots left" are sales pressure, not scarcity.

### 4. Is the speaking slot editorially selected or included in a sponsorship — and will it be labeled sponsored on the agenda?

Pay-to-speak isn't automatically disqualifying, but it changes what you're buying and how the room receives it. The dodge is the signal.

- **Good:** a straight answer either way, agenda labeling confirmed in writing.
- **Walk away:** you were "selected as a speaker" and the package costs more than a general ticket. That inversion is the whole business model.

### 5. How many attendees last year held VP Engineering, CTO, Head of Talent or equivalent titles, and at what company sizes?

The number the fit decision turns on. See your `audience-fit.md`.

- **Good:** a real breakdown with enough buyer density to justify the spend.
- **Walk away:** overwhelmingly individual-contributor engineers and students.

### 6. Can you send the attendee company list from last year, or registered-to-date for this one?

Company names, not individuals. Check it against hiring partners and the target list.

- **Good:** a company-level list, or at minimum last year's logo wall.
- **Walk away:** they offer to *sell* an attendee email list. That ends it — legitimate organizers state publicly they never sell attendee contact data, and the sender often isn't affiliated with the event at all.

### 7. What's the structured way to meet those people — curated 1:1s, a hosted-buyer program, a meeting room?

- **Good:** a meeting program with booking windows, and a stated average number of meetings sponsors got last year.
- **Walk away:** the only offering is a table in a hall and a logo on a banner.

### 8. Which legal entity issues the invoice, and can you send a W-9 and certificate of insurance?

Routine for a real business, a hard stop for a shell.

- **Good:** W-9 and COI from the same entity on the contract and the website, payable by card or ACH to a domestic account.
- **Walk away:** wire, crypto or Zelle only; a personal-name payee; a bank country that doesn't match the entity.

### 9. Have your listed keynotes signed speaker agreements?

- **Good:** yes, and the speakers confirm it when asked directly.
- **Walk away:** "confirmed pending final scheduling" on names who don't respond. Expect roughly a third of advertised speakers to no-show at weak events.

### 10. What happens to our money if the event is postponed, moved, or cancelled?

- **Good:** a written clause with a payment schedule and defined organizer-cancellation terms.
- **Walk away:** "that's never happened," or a deposit non-refundable under all circumstances.

### 11. Are sessions recorded, and who owns the footage?

- **Good:** professionally recorded, we get the file, free to cut and post.
- **Walk away:** no recording, or the organizer keeps exclusive rights behind their own paywall.

# Grading the reply

The response is evidence, often better than anything on the website. Read it on four axes and put the read in the dossier — the user will want it when they defend the decision.

**What arrived versus what was deflected.** Score item by item: attached, answered in prose, deferred to a call, or ignored. A real organizer sends the report and the rate card in one reply because both are sitting in a folder. Selective deflection is the tell — note *which* questions got redirected, because it's almost always the numbers and the price.

**Speed and shape.** Same-day with attachments is the healthy pattern. A week, delivered in fragments, is a different signal. Watch for a second sender appearing with a different signature block once you engage; that's a handoff to a closer, and it means the first email was prospecting rather than a relationship.

**Whether numbers survive contact.** Compare what comes back against what the desk check found. Attendance figures that shrink when asked for a breakdown, "sold out" that becomes "filling fast," an edition count that quietly drops — any of these is worth more than the original claim.

**Whether they answer the question asked.** Answering an adjacent, easier question is a soft form of deflection and easy to miss when the reply is warm and long. "How many VP-level attendees?" answered with total registrations is a non-answer.

Two outcomes deserve naming explicitly in the dossier. A reply that produces the report, the rate card and two references in one message is a strong positive signal and should raise the recommendation, not just fail to lower it. A reply that produces none of them, however friendly, has answered the question.
